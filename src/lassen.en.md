## *Lassen*

*Lassen* is an irregular verb that covers English *let*, *leave*, *make*,
*have*, and *stop*. To choose the German for one of those English verbs, see
[How to say *let*, *leave*, *make*, *have*, and *stop*](lassen-uebersetzen.en.md).
For why one verb covers all of them, see
[Why *lassen* means both *let* and *make*](lassen-hintergrund.en.md).

### Forms

*Lassen* is a strong verb and forms the Perfekt with *haben*.[^duden_lassen]

| Form        | German                                                                |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Präsens     | *ich lasse*, *du lässt*, *er lässt*, *wir lassen*, *ihr lasst*        |
| Imperativ   | *lass* (or *lasse*), *lasst*, *lassen Sie*                            |
| Präteritum  | *ließ*                                                                |
| Partizip II | *gelassen*, or *lassen* after an infinitive (see [Perfekt](#perfekt)) |

### Meanings

| Meaning        | Example                              | English                      |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| to abstain     | *Lass das!*                          | Stop that!                   |
| to leave as is | *Lass das Fenster offen.*            | Leave the window open.       |
| to allow       | *Lass mich rein.*                    | Let me in.                   |
| to cause       | *Ich lasse mein Fahrrad reparieren.* | I'm having my bike repaired. |
| to be possible | *Die Tür lässt sich öffnen.*         | The door can be opened.      |
| to suggest     | *Lass uns gehen.*                    | Let's go.                    |

Without a second verb, *lassen* usually means *abstain* or *leave as is*. With
an infinitive, it usually means *allow* or *cause*. The infinitive takes no
*zu*: *Sie lässt mich gehen* (she lets me go). *Lass das!* stops something in
progress; *Lass es* (drop it) talks someone out of a
plan.[^yourdailygerman_lassen1]

### *Lassen* with an Infinitive

With an infinitive, *lassen* can mean *allow* or *cause*. Context decides which,
and some sentences allow both.[^nadathur_lauer]

| German                                            | Reading    | English                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| *Die Eltern lassen die Kinder länger aufbleiben.* | permission | The parents let the children stay up later. |
| *Die Lehrerin lässt die Klasse nachsitzen.*       | order      | The teacher keeps the class in detention.   |
| *Sie lässt die Kinder fernsehen.*                 | either     | She lets / has the children watch TV.       |

*Erlauben* (permit) and *dafür sorgen, dass* (make sure that) carry only one of
the two readings.

The person doing the action is in the accusative: *Ich lasse ihn arbeiten.*
Without that person, *lassen* with an infinitive means arranging a service. This
form is very common and neutral.[^yourdailygerman_lassen1]

| German                                | English                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| *Ich lasse mein Fahrrad reparieren.*  | I'm getting my bike repaired. |
| *Ich lasse mich massieren.*           | I'm getting a massage.        |
| *Ich lasse mich scheiden.*            | I'm getting a divorce.        |
| *Ich lasse mir die Haare schneiden.*  | I'm getting a haircut.        |
| *Ich lasse mir die Rechnung bringen.* | I'll ask for the bill.        |

The reflexive pronoun is accusative when the subject is the object of the action
(*mich massieren*) and dative when the sentence has another object (*mir die
Haare schneiden*). *Von* names the person who does it: *Ich lasse mir die Haare
von meiner Schwester schneiden* (my sister cuts my hair).

### *Sich lassen*

The meaning of *sich lassen* depends on the subject. Textbooks call the
possibility reading a Passiversatzform; for why that label can mislead, see
[Is *sich lassen* a passive?](lassen-hintergrund.en.md#is-sich-lassen-a-passive)

With an inanimate subject, *sich lassen* means that something is possible. It
corresponds to a passive with *können*.

| Passive with *können*             | *sich lassen*                     | English                  |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| *Die Tür kann geöffnet werden.*   | *Die Tür lässt sich öffnen.*      | The door opens.          |
| *Das Problem kann gelöst werden.* | *Das Problem lässt sich lösen.*   | The problem is solvable. |
| *Das kann leicht erklärt werden.* | *Das lässt sich leicht erklären.* | That's easy to explain.  |

It often appears with *nicht*, *leicht*, *schwer*, or *gut*. The impersonal form
takes *es*: *Hier lässt es sich leben* (life is good here).[^duden_lassen]

With an animate subject, *sich lassen* means permission.

| German                             | English                     |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| *Die Katze lässt sich streicheln.* | The cat lets people pet it. |
| *Ich lasse mich gern überzeugen.*  | I'm open to persuasion.     |

### *Lass uns*

*Lass uns* plus an infinitive is a suggestion, like English *let's*. The form of
*lassen* depends on who is addressed.[^duden_lassen]

| Addressee | Form                    |
| --------- | ----------------------- |
| *du*      | *Lass uns gehen.*       |
| *ihr*     | *Lasst uns gehen.*      |
| *Sie*     | *Lassen Sie uns gehen.* |

*Gehen wir!* makes the same suggestion without *lassen*. The infinitive can be
*lassen* itself: *Lass es uns lassen* (let's skip it).[^yourdailygerman_lassen1]

### Perfekt

In conversation, the Perfekt is more common than *ließ*. Without an infinitive,
*lassen* takes the participle *gelassen*. With an infinitive, it takes the
Ersatzinfinitiv *lassen*, as the modal verbs do.[^duden_lassen]

| Construction       | Participle  | Example                                     | English                    |
| ------------------ | ----------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| without infinitive | *gelassen*  | *Ich habe den Schlüssel zu Hause gelassen.* | I left the key at home.    |
| with infinitive    | *lassen*    | *Ich habe dich schlafen lassen.*            | I let you sleep.           |
| with infinitive    | *lassen*    | *Ich habe mir die Haare schneiden lassen.*  | I had my hair cut.         |
| prefix verb        | Partizip II | *Er hat das Seil runtergelassen.*           | He lowered the rope.       |
| with infinitive    | *lassen*    | *Er hat das Seil runterhängen lassen.*      | He let the rope hang down. |

With *liegen*, *stehen*, and *fallen*, both forms occur: *Ich habe das Glas
fallen lassen* and *fallen gelassen* (I dropped the
glass).[^yourdailygerman_lassen2] Duden lists *gelassen* after an infinitive as
the rarer variant.[^duden_lassen]

In a subordinate clause, the auxiliary goes before the two
infinitives.[^yourdailygerman_lassen2]

| Clause      | Example                                   |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| main        | *Ich habe sie schlafen lassen.*           |
| subordinate | *..., dass ich sie habe schlafen lassen.* |
| not         | *..., dass ich sie schlafen lassen habe.* |

The modal verbs follow the same order: *..., weil ich habe arbeiten müssen*
(because I had to work).

### Prefix Verbs

These are the common prefix verbs built on *lassen*.[^yourdailygerman_lassen2]

| Verb                       | Meaning                        | Example                               |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| *anlassen*, *auflassen*    | leave on, leave open           | *Lass das Radio an.*                  |
| *reinlassen*, *rauslassen* | let in, let out                | *Lass die Katze raus.*                |
| *weglassen*                | leave out                      | *Ich lasse die Butter weg.*           |
| *auslassen*                | skip                           | *Ich lasse das Frühstück aus.*        |
| *zulassen*                 | allow, approve                 | *Das Amt lässt den Impfstoff zu.*     |
| *überlassen*               | leave to, hand over            | *Das überlasse ich dir.*              |
| *hinterlassen*             | leave behind (message, estate) | *Hinterlassen Sie eine Nachricht.*    |
| *entlassen*                | lay off, release               | *Die Firma entlässt 200 Mitarbeiter.* |
| *erlassen*                 | enact; waive                   | *Das Parlament erlässt ein Gesetz.*   |
| *belassen*                 | formal for *leave as is*       | *Wir belassen es dabei.*              |
| [*verlassen*](#verlassen)  | leave (a place or person)      | *Er hat seine Frau verlassen.*        |

### *Verlassen*

| Use                           | Example                              | English              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------- |
| go away from (+ Akk.)         | *Er hat seine Frau verlassen.*       | He left his wife.    |
| *sich verlassen auf* (+ Akk.) | *Du kannst dich auf mich verlassen.* | You can count on me. |

*Verlassen* always takes an accusative object. In everyday speech it sounds
formal.[^yourdailygerman_lassen2] *Sich verlassen auf* keeps the reflexive
pronoun: *Du kannst dich auf mich verlassen*, not *Du kannst auf mich
verlassen*. The adjectives *zuverlässig* and *unzuverlässig* (reliable,
unreliable) come from it.

### Idioms

| Expression                                         | English                          |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| *Lass dir Zeit.*                                   | Take your time.                  |
| *Lass es lieber sein.*                             | Better skip it.                  |
| *Das lasse ich mir gefallen.*                      | I could get used to that.        |
| *Das kann sich sehen lassen.*                      | That's impressive.               |
| *Das lässt sich hören.*                            | That sounds reasonable.          |
| *Über Geschmack lässt sich nicht streiten.*        | There's no accounting for taste. |
| *Die Kritiken lassen auf einen guten Film hoffen.* | The reviews promise a good film. |
