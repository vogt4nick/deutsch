## Subjective Use of Modal Verbs

Modal verbs have two readings. In their objective use, they describe the
subject's ability, permission, obligation, or intent: *Sie muss daheim bleiben*
(she has to stay home). In their subjective use, the speaker judges how likely a
statement is or passes on someone else's claim: *Sie muss daheim geblieben sein*
(she must have stayed home). Learners pick up the objective use early. Many
never master the subjective use, even at B2 or C1, and fall back on plain
declarative sentences instead.[^fischengeil]

### Inference

With *müssen*, *dürfen*, and *können*, the speaker rates how likely something
is.

| Form       | Judgment                        | Example                                        | English                                  |
| ---------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| muss       | near certain, logical inference | *Es muss in der Nacht geregnet haben.*         | It must have rained overnight.           |
| müsste     | very likely, expected           | *Der Zug müsste gleich kommen.*                | The train should be here any minute.     |
| dürfte     | very likely                     | *Das dürfte das Problem sein.*                 | That's probably the problem.             |
| kann       | possible                        | *Das kann sein.*                               | That may be.                             |
| könnte     | possible, more tentative        | *Das könnte ein Missverständnis gewesen sein.* | That might have been a misunderstanding. |
| muss nicht | not necessarily                 | *Das muss nicht stimmen.*                      | That isn't necessarily true.             |
| kann nicht | all but ruled out               | *Das kann nicht stimmen.*                      | That can't be right.                     |

*Dürfen* carries this meaning only in Konjunktiv II: *Das dürfte stimmen*, never
*Das darf stimmen*.[^politt_duerfte] Negation flips the ranking: *kann nicht*
rules something out, while *muss nicht* only leaves it open.

*Mögen* expresses concession rather than probability, and an *aber* usually
follows: *Das mag sein, aber es hilft uns nicht* (that may be, but it doesn't
help us). With estimates it marks uncertainty: *Sie mag etwa vierzig sein* (she
might be about forty). Both uses sound formal.[^politt_mag]

### Reported Claims

With *sollen* and *wollen*, the speaker passes on a claim without vouching for
it.

*Sollen* reports what others say about the subject, like English *is said to*,
*supposedly*, or *reportedly*: *Er soll Arzt gewesen sein* (he is said to have
been a doctor). *Das Fußballspiel soll abgesagt worden sein* (the soccer game
was supposedly cancelled). News reports use it routinely to mark unconfirmed
information: *Der Täter soll über die Grenze geflohen sein* (the suspect
reportedly fled across the border).[^mortelmans]

*Wollen* reports what the subject claims about themselves, like English *claims
to*: *Er will nichts gesehen haben* (he claims not to have seen anything). *Sie
will im Lotto gewonnen haben* (she claims to have won the lottery). It usually
signals the speaker's doubt. *Behaupten* with Konjunktiv I sounds neutral and
detached instead: *Sie behauptet, sie habe im Lotto gewonnen.* In casual speech,
people tend to say: *Sie sagt, sie hat im Lotto gewonnen.*

### Formation

The modal verb stays in the present tense or Konjunktiv II because the judgment
happens at the moment of speaking. The infinitive carries the time of the event.

| Event         | Infinitive                 | Example                                |
| ------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| simultaneous  | present infinitive         | *Er soll Arzt sein.*                   |
| past          | perfect infinitive         | *Er soll Arzt gewesen sein.*           |
| past, passive | perfect passive infinitive | *Das Spiel soll abgesagt worden sein.* |

With a present infinitive, the sentence is often ambiguous: *Er muss im Büro
sein* can mean he is required to be at the office or that he must be there. Only
the perfect infinitive makes the subjective reading unambiguous. That yields the
key contrast:

| Use        | Form             | Example                     | Meaning              |
| ---------- | ---------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------- |
| objective  | *hat ... müssen* | *Er hat arbeiten müssen.*   | He had to work.      |
| subjective | *muss ... haben* | *Er muss gearbeitet haben.* | He must have worked. |

In narrative, the modal verb can appear in the Präteritum when the judgment
itself lies in the past: *Die Straße war nass. Es musste in der Nacht geregnet
haben* (the street was wet; it must have rained overnight).

### Translation Traps

English inferences about the past are easy to mistranslate.

| English                                    | German                                         | Not                                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| *That might have been a misunderstanding.* | *Das könnte ein Missverständnis gewesen sein.* | *Das hätte ein Missverständnis sein können.* |
| *It must have rained.*                     | *Es muss geregnet haben.*                      | *Es hat regnen müssen.*                      |
| *The game was supposedly cancelled.*       | *Das Spiel soll abgesagt worden sein.*         | *Das Spiel ist abgesagt.*                    |
| *She claims to have won.*                  | *Sie will gewonnen haben.*                     | *Sie hat gewonnen.*                          |

*Hätte ... sein können* is counterfactual: it means *could have been, but
wasn't*.

### Register

Not every form is equally at home in conversation. *Das muss so sein*, *Das kann
nicht stimmen*, *Das dürfte schwierig werden*, and *Er will nichts gesehen
haben* come up constantly. *Sollen* and *wollen* with a perfect infinitive, as
well as *mögen*, lean toward writing, the news, and formal speech. In
conversation, many native speakers would rather say *Ich glaube, das war ein
Missverständnis* (I think that was a misunderstanding) or *Ich habe gehört, er
war Arzt* (I heard he was a doctor).[^fischengeil] Recognizing the subjective
forms makes you a more precise reader. Using them lets you express inference and
hearsay more concisely, but don't pack every sentence with them.
