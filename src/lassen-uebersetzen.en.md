## How to Say *let*, *leave*, *make*, *have*, and *stop*

German often uses *lassen* where English says *let*, *leave*, *make*, *have*, or
*stop*, but not always. Find the English verb you want, then use the German
pattern. For forms and grammar, see the [*lassen* reference](lassen.en.md).

### *Leave*

If you're going away, use *gehen* in everyday speech.

| English                 | German                                   |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| I'm leaving.            | *Ich gehe jetzt.*                        |
| I'm heading out.        | *Ich mach mich auf den Weg.*             |
| We left the bar at two. | *Wir sind um zwei aus der Bar gegangen.* |

*Verlassen* also means going away, but it needs an accusative object and sounds
formal: *Wir haben die Bar um zwei verlassen* sounds like a police
report.[^yourdailygerman_lassen2] Use it for leaving a person or in formal
writing: *Er hat seine Frau verlassen.* Don't say *Ich verlasse* without an
object.

If you're leaving something somewhere or in a state, use *lassen*.

| English                 | German                                        |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| I left my keys at home. | *Ich habe meine Schlüssel zu Hause gelassen.* |
| Leave the window open.  | *Lass das Fenster offen.*                     |
| That leaves me cold.    | *Das lässt mich kalt.*                        |

Not *Ich habe meine Schlüssel zu Hause verlassen*: that means you walked out on
your keys. For *leave on*, *leave out*, *leave behind*, and similar, use the
[prefix verbs](lassen.en.md#prefix-verbs).

If something is left over, use *noch*, *da*, or *übrig*.

| English                 | German                     |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- |
| There's one beer left.  | *Es ist noch ein Bier da.* |
| Is there any soup left? | *Ist noch Suppe übrig?*    |

Not *Ein Bier ist gelassen*.[^yourdailygerman_lassen2]

### *Let*

To let someone do something, use *lassen* with an infinitive and no *zu*.

| English                 | German                       |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Let me in.              | *Lass mich rein.*            |
| She let me go.          | *Sie hat mich gehen lassen.* |
| Let the children sleep. | *Lass die Kinder schlafen.*  |

If it must be clear that this is permission and not an order, use *erlauben*
with a dative and *zu*: *Sie erlaubt mir, das Auto zu nehmen.*

For *let's*, use *lass uns*, matching the person you're talking to: *Lass uns
gehen* (du), *Lasst uns gehen* (ihr), *Lassen Sie uns gehen* (Sie). *Gehen wir!*
is often more natural.[^duden_lassen]

### *Make*

To make someone do something, use *lassen* with an infinitive.

| English                               | German                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| The teacher made the class stay late. | *Die Lehrerin ließ die Klasse nachsitzen.* |
| The boss made him check the files.    | *Der Chef ließ ihn die Akten prüfen.*      |

*Lassen* can also mean *let*. If the sentence must be read as an order, use
*dafür sorgen, dass*: *Sie sorgt dafür, dass die Kinder früh ins Bett gehen.*

If you talked someone into it, use *dazu bringen* with a *zu*-infinitive. For a
reaction like laughing or crying, use *zum ... bringen*.[^duden_bringen]

| English                         | German                                                |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| She got him to change his mind. | *Sie hat ihn dazu gebracht, seine Meinung zu ändern.* |
| That made me laugh.             | *Das hat mich zum Lachen gebracht.*                   |
| The film made her cry.          | *Der Film hat sie zum Weinen gebracht.*               |

If *make* means putting someone in a state, use *machen* with an adjective: *Das
macht mich neugierig* (that makes me curious).[^duden_machen] *Lassen* with an
adjective means leaving someone in a state: *Das lässt mich kalt* (that leaves
me cold).[^duden_kaltlassen]

### *Have*

To have something done, use *lassen* with an infinitive and leave out the person
who does it.

| English                       | German                                                          |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I'm getting my bike repaired. | *Ich lasse mein Fahrrad reparieren.*                            |
| I'm getting a haircut.        | *Ich lasse mir die Haare schneiden.*                            |
| I had my hair cut.            | *Ich habe mir die Haare schneiden lassen.*                      |
| My sister cut my hair.        | *Ich habe mir die Haare von meiner Schwester schneiden lassen.* |

Use *mich* when you are the object of the action (*Ich lasse mich massieren*)
and *mir* when the sentence has another object (*mir die Haare schneiden*).

Not *Ich hatte meine Haare geschnitten*: that means you had cut your hair
yourself.[^yourdailygerman_lassen2]

To have someone do something, name that person in the accusative: *Ich lasse ihn
arbeiten.*

### *Stop*

To tell someone to stop, use *Lass das!* for something in progress and *Lass es*
(drop it) to talk them out of a plan.[^yourdailygerman_lassen1] *Hör auf!* also
works for something in progress.[^yourdailygerman_aufhoeren]

To stop doing something, use *aufhören* with *mit* and a noun, or with a
*zu*-infinitive. Don't drop the *mit*.[^yourdailygerman_aufhoeren] For habits,
*lassen* also works.

| English               | German                          |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| I'm quitting smoking. | *Ich höre mit dem Rauchen auf.* |
| Stop talking.         | *Hör auf zu reden.*             |
| She quit smoking.     | *Sie hat das Rauchen gelassen.* |

To stop a vehicle, use *anhalten*, not *aufhören*.[^yourdailygerman_aufhoeren]

### *Can be done*

German also uses *lassen* for English *can be done* and *-able*. Use *sich
lassen* with an infinitive.

| English                  | German                                 |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| The problem is solvable. | *Das Problem lässt sich lösen.*        |
| That's easy to explain.  | *Das lässt sich leicht erklären.*      |
| The window won't open.   | *Das Fenster lässt sich nicht öffnen.* |

The passive with *können* is also correct but longer: *Das Problem kann gelöst
werden.* With a person or animal as subject, *sich lassen* means permission
instead: *Die Katze lässt sich streicheln* (the cat lets people pet it).
